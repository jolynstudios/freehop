Every option on this page can put people in a call. They differ in one question that decides your bill: **when two people cannot connect directly, where do their audio and video go?**

- **Peer-to-peer libraries** (PeerJS, Trystero, plain WebRTC) send media directly when they can. When they can't, they need a TURN relay, and someone pays for every byte it carries.
- **SFU servers** (LiveKit, Jitsi, mediasoup) route group media through a server. Jitsi also tries a direct route in two-person calls. Server forwarding makes large rooms, recording and livestreams possible, and makes the server's bandwidth a running cost.
- **Hosted APIs** run those servers for you and charge per minute or per gigabyte.
- **Freehop** keeps media between the people in the call. When a direct route fails, it uses machines that already belong to the session: a participant's desktop gateway, the host node, or a participant who forwards. [Gates](./concepts/gates.mdx) pass sealed signalling only. You may also operate a session gateway and pay for its media traffic.

Freehop’s gates carry no media. Participants and session gateways still use bandwidth and compute; a gateway you host can add those costs to your bill. The 2-to-8-person range is the intended use, not enforced room admission; see [room limits](./limits.mdx#small-rooms).

## Which one fits

**Freehop fits when:**

- calls are small groups of 2 to 8 people: meeting rooms, in-app calls, shared workspaces, communities or games;
- you don't want a relay bill that grows with every call, and you accept that a pair with no route inside the session is reported as `unreachable`, or you opt into your own TURN relay for just those pairs;
- people use your app in the browser and on the desktop, so desktop participants can open a front door for everyone.

**An SFU (LiveKit, Jitsi, mediasoup) fits when:**

- rooms have dozens or hundreds of people, or one person broadcasts to many;
- you need recording, server-side processing, transcription or AI agents in the call;
- you want media to pass through your server and budget for that bandwidth; successful connections still depend on transport support and firewall rules.

**A thin peer-to-peer library (PeerJS, Trystero, plain WebRTC) fits when:**

- you want one-to-one calls or data channels with the least code;
- you are happy to run (and pay for) your own TURN server for the pairs that can't connect directly.

**A hosted API fits when:**

- you want nothing to operate and a vendor contract, and per-minute pricing works for your margins.

## What Freehop does not do

Being honest about the trade-offs is part of the comparison:

- **No large-room capacity benchmark.** Freehop is a mesh: every person sends to every other person. Larger rooms need separate load testing; an SFU reduces each participant's upload requirements.
- **No built-in recording or server-side media processing.** Gates cannot record media they never receive. Receiving participants can record it, including a participant forwarding decoded tracks; gateway relays carry encrypted packets.
- **No guarantee on locked-down networks.** A network that can reach only your server has no route for media without your server carrying it. Freehop reports `unreachable` instead of paying for a relay, unless you opt into a TURN relay that network can reach. See [limits](./limits.mdx).
- **Tested in a developer-built home lab, not field-proven.** See [network test results](./results.mdx).

## Sources

Checked against each project's own documentation and source code on 2 October 2026. Pricing and service availability can change:

- [coturn](https://github.com/coturn/coturn): TURN server, BSD-3-Clause.
- [PeerJS](https://peerjs.com) and its [repository](https://github.com/peers/peerjs): MIT and PeerServer cloud; the maintainers' [5 December 2023 announcement](https://github.com/orgs/peers/discussions/1172) ended the free TURN service. Some default settings still list the old TURN addresses; they are not a supplied service you should rely on.
- [Trystero](https://github.com/dmotz/trystero#readme): MIT; Nostr is the default strategy, TURN is configured separately, and a password protects signalling with a private key.
- [LiveKit](https://github.com/livekit/livekit): Apache-2.0 SFU; [embedded TURN](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/self-hosting/deployment/), [end-to-end encryption](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/encryption/), and [Cloud pricing](https://livekit.com/pricing).
- [Jitsi Meet](https://github.com/jitsi/jitsi-meet) and [Jitsi Videobridge](https://github.com/jitsi/jitsi-videobridge): Apache-2.0; [security and two-person P2P mode](https://jitsi.org/security/), [P2P configuration](https://github.com/jitsi/jitsi-meet/blob/master/config.js), and [JaaS](https://jaas.8x8.vc/).
- [mediasoup](https://github.com/versatica/mediasoup): ISC; [Node.js and Rust integrations](https://mediasoup.org/documentation/v3/).
- [simple-peer](https://github.com/feross/simple-peer): MIT; configurable ICE servers and mesh groups built by the application.
- Hosted APIs: [Daily](https://www.daily.co/pricing/), [Agora](https://www.agora.io/en/pricing/), [Twilio Video](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/video/pricing), [Cloudflare Realtime](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/).
- Native game networks: [Steam Datagram Relay](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/multiplayer/steamdatagramrelay) and [Epic Online Services P2P](https://dev.epicgames.com/docs/epic-online-services/multiplayer/nat-p2p-interface/p2p-reference).

Something out of date or wrong? [Open an issue](https://github.com/jolynstudios/freehop/issues) and we will correct it.
