## Freehop does not charge you

Freehop is open-source software under the Apache-2.0 license. There is no Freehop subscription, per-seat fee or per-minute media charge. The provider prices and estimates on this page describe other services or infrastructure you choose to run; they are not fees charged by Freehop.

Your deployment can still have costs. You may pay to host your application backend and gate, and for any bandwidth or compute used by a gateway or host node that runs on infrastructure you pay for. If the gateway belongs to a participant, that participant contributes the upload. A gate carries sealed signalling only; it does not relay media.

## Estimate a separate TURN relay bill

If you use a conventional TURN provider, the provider may charge for relayed traffic. The interactive estimate below compares that hypothetical TURN egress with Freehop's gate signalling traffic. It does not include Freehop software fees (there are none), gate/backend hosting, or the cost of a session gateway that you host yourself.

There is no relay percentage that can predict every product. Public figures are tied to particular services, network mixes and measurement periods, so they are not a reliable default for your app. The calculator's relay-share input is a scenario you set, not an industry benchmark. Measure the routes in your own user and network mix before using the result for a budget.

TURN pricing depends on the provider and plan. As one current example, Cloudflare lists a 1,000 GB monthly free allowance for Realtime TURN, then $0.05 per GB of egress for standalone TURN; it is free when used with Cloudflare Realtime SFU. The calculator lets you enter a rate and an allowance, but does not model every provider's bundle, tax, discount or billing rule. [Cloudflare TURN pricing and billing details](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/faq/). This is a Cloudflare service price, not a Freehop price.

## What moves over the network?

The arithmetic below uses Freehop's default media bitrates and decimal units (1 GB = 1,000 MB). These are traffic volumes, not prices:

| Item | Data volume |
|---|---:|
| One person-hour of relayed audio at 32 kbit/s, delivered by the relay | 14.4 MB |
| One person-hour of relayed audio plus video at 32 + 300 kbit/s | 149.4 MB |
| One call setup through a Freehop gate, per peer pair | about 15 to 35 KB |

Connected pairs move most signalling onto their own data channels. Keepalives, newcomer discovery and recovery still use the gate. In the [home-lab network tests](../results.mdx), a gate's total for a whole simulated scenario, retries included, was 30 to 210 KB.

## Who carries the media?

| Path | Who carries the media | Where bandwidth or compute costs may land |
|---|---|---|
| `direct` | The two endpoints communicate directly | Each participant uses their own connection |
| `gateway` | A desktop participant's gateway | That participant's upload, unless you run the gateway |
| `relay` | A gateway belonging to a session member | The gateway owner's connection or hosting bill |
| `bridged` | A participant that forwards and re-encodes | That participant's upload and CPU |
| `unreachable` | Nobody; no media flows | No media traffic is sent |

Freehop avoids sending media through its gates. It cannot make bandwidth free: if you run the session host or gateway on your own paid server, you pay that server's normal bandwidth and compute costs. Forwarded video is capped at 200 kbit/s, and gateways have per-allocation, per-room and overall quotas.

## What you need to run

You can run a gate, or use a public WebTorrent tracker as a room's gate, as the [live call](/demo) on this site does. A gate needs a public HTTPS/WebSocket endpoint and, if you enable STUN, a UDP port. Hosting and traffic for your backend and gate depend on your provider and usage. See [Gate service](../sdk/gate-service.mdx).
